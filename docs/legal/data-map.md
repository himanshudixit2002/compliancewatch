# Data map

> Draft - to be reviewed by a lawyer and by the Platform team. Retention periods are the
> engineering assumptions from the design guide (section 16) until counsel confirms them.

Version: 0.2-draft

Every personal field, where it lives, why it is held and how long. "Deletion" means the
erasure cascade that runs after `tenant.deletion.requested` (deadline 30 days), and the
"Erasure handler" column names what each service's consumer does to the field
(docs/runbooks/data-requests.md). It runs only while the flag `identity.tenant_erasure` is on,
and only for the event identity sent for the tenant's open deletion request: every consumer
checks it with identity first and refuses any other (and the internal tenant), with an audit
row. Identity sends the request twice, the second time once every service has answered and an
access token issued before the erasure has expired, and each service keeps an erased marker
(`erased_tenant`: the tenant id, when, the event's id) after which its routes answer 410 and its
consumers drop the tenant's events.

## Erasure exceptions

Draft - to be reviewed by a lawyer. What a tenant's erasure keeps, and why:

- `audit.event` is kept for 7 years, immutable, masked for personal identifiers when each row is
  written. The erasure itself is recorded there: a `tenant.erased` row per service and pass
  (identity's lists the accounts of another sign-in provider, by user id, provider and subject,
  for the operator to delete there), a `tenant.erasure_refused` row for every event a service
  refused, and the deletion request's `data_request.*` rows.
- Consent records are pseudonymised, not deleted: the subject becomes `erased:` and the first 32
  hex digits of HMAC-SHA256(pepper, tenant|subject), the evidence is emptied and `recorded_by` is
  nulled. The pepper (`CW_IDENTITY_ERASURE_PEPPER`) is a secret held outside the database:
  without it a pseudonym cannot be traced back by trying every phone number or address, as an
  unkeyed hash allowed; whoever holds it can still test a candidate value, which is why this is
  pseudonymisation and not anonymisation. Counsel must still sign the formula off. The record
  still proves that consent was given or withdrawn, when and under which notice.
- Billing: the provider's customer and subscription ids, the plans, quantities and dates, the
  subscription starts and the masked webhook ledger are kept for the tax records (8 years
  assumed); the billing contact's email address is pseudonymised the same way and the name
  emptied, and the checkout links are emptied [lawyer].
- The data request and the erased tenant (id, kind, region, status) are kept as the record that
  the request was made and answered; every service keeps its erased marker (`erased_tenant`).
- The llm-gateway's `cost_ledger` keeps the tenant id with each call's feature, tokens and cost
  for 13 months, and no personal data; it has no erasure handler.
- Regulatory data (the rulebook's documents, rules, candidates and their review) belongs to no
  tenant and is kept; the rulebook's erasure lists it.
- Notification preferences: an opt-out is kept as the record that the person asked to stop, which
  must outlive the account; an opt-in of an address another tenant still holds is kept, since
  consent is honoured for every tenant that sends to the address. Neither keeps its reference to
  the erased tenant. Suppressions (bounced or complaining addresses) are kept for every tenant.
- Pending events in a service's outbox, the erasure's own `tenant.data.erased` among them, are
  kept until the relay publishes them. Nothing prunes the outbox on its own: an operator does
  (docs/runbooks/outbox-relay.md, "Pruning"); the events hold ids and counts, no personal data.
- Outside the databases: the profile's not-applicable answers appended to
  `CW_PROFILE_EVAL_CASES_PATH` (a local eval seed, off by default), the Kafka topics and the
  Temporal histories above, which expire with their retention, and the backups, which age out
  with theirs; the runbook covers them.

Data residency: the design assumes the Mumbai region (ap-south-1) for every store; Meta and
the payment provider process outside it under their own terms. Email stays off
(`CW_EMAIL_ENABLED`) until a sending domain in the region is verified with SES or another SMTP
provider.

Cross-border model processing (Draft - to be reviewed by a lawyer): every call the llm-gateway
makes to a real model sends text outside India, since no model it routes to runs inference in
India. The text is regulator documents and, for an answer, the user's question with its evidence,
with GSTINs, PANs, Aadhaar numbers, phone numbers and email addresses masked first where the
patterns recognise them; a name or another detail in a question's free text is not. The providers
are asked to retain nothing (zero data retention), and the traces of the calls hold the same masked
text. `CW_LLM_RESIDENCY=india_only` stops every such call and keeps the text out of the traces;
log lines and trace spans still go wherever their collectors run. Whether the product runs that
way, or keeps the calls and says so, is open (ADR-020).
